Every finding faced two independent skeptics — one reading the code to refute it, one trying to reproduce it by running something — both instructed to default to refuted. All 14 verified findings survived, which is itself a signal: the lanes were not padding.

The change set is not sound. Three defects change reported numbers, one of them on a shipped candidate. A fourth makes the ADJ-11 one-accounting gate self-fulfilling in both the unit test and the shipped validator. The tree ships with five failing tests in its own QIF suite.

The one that matters: ADJ-14 sizes a chiplet grouping the machine does not build

fws_eval.py:3259 deals stages to chiplets in contiguous blocks and hands that grouping to the engine and fabric derivations. The lowering assigns every shared-digital op round-robin: engines[chip_id % len(engines)] at program/fws_build.py:816, and the same modulo at :1348. Whenever shared_chiplets < stages, the two disagree — the engine is widened for a machine nobody builds.

It is not sign-safe. Checked-in candidate c012 of the Granite sweep ships vector_lanes: 28175; the grouping the DAG actually builds needs 32,332. That chiplet runs 1.874 µs against a 1.7 µs analog pace — under-provisioned by 10% against ADJ-14's own criterion — while the shipped disclosure states the criterion "is met."

No headline number moves: Granite ships one chiplet per stage, and Qwen's stages are homogeneous. But the frontier ADJ-14 exists to explore is scored on a machine that is not built, and DECISIONS.md:377 ("a chiplet serves neighbours in the pipeline and reads its operands locally") is false of every shared machine the repo builds.

Confirmed — reproduced or traced precisely

  1. Contiguous vs round-robin chiplet assignmentcritical

    program/fws_build.py:816  vs  fws_eval.py:3259

    As above. It also makes _stage_chiplet_groups return fewer groups than there are chiplets when chiplets > stages/2 — 10 stages over 8 chiplets yields 5 groups — so all 8 chiplets are built 16.2% too wide while only 5 are sized.

  2. The Mamba depthwise conv is charged nowheremajor

    fws_eval.py:3421 · fws_eval.py:2817 · fws_mapping.py:411

    Conv dimensions are read only from model.short_conv, which is None for a pure-SSM model, so digital_pool_sizing() receives conv_kernel = 0 and every shipped report carries conv_lanes: 0. The mapper drops the conv weights from the tile set because they "run on the per-macro pool", and the scan work law has no conv term — so the work is charged on neither side.

    Separately, the state bill counts only the h-state and omits the per-stream conv window of d_inner × (d_conv−1): 3.15 MB per stage on Falcon-Mamba, +18.75% on a store that ships at 856.58 mm², while uncovered_terms is []. The run's own disclosure says "'absorbed' is a sized claim here, not a hope." For this model nothing was sized.

  3. Five tests are red on the shipped treemajor1 fixed

    tests/test_qif_atlas.py (×3) · tests/test_qif_dse_allocation.py (×2)

    Two root causes. ADJ-14 restored shared_chiplets to AXIS_TARGETS without adding it to the one-axis-one-field map at tests/test_qif_dse_allocation.py:253 — fixed. And the Granite card was rewritten to its selected design while the fixtures that copy from it were not: fixture_frontier_granite.json:595 reports 62,661.48 tokens/s where the shipped atlas now says 65,325.09, and that fixture is user-reachable at atlas.html?frontier=fixture_frontier_granite.json. fws_cim_granite_tiny_dse.yaml:53 still declares 640 slots against the shipped 484 — the exact drift the failing test's own docstring says it exists to catch.

  4. The ADJ-11 one-accounting gate compares a value with a copy of itselfmajor

    tools/fws_qif_dse.py:1496 · tests/test_qif_frontier.py:574 · validate_fws_cim_vs_optima.py:2773

    The DSE assigns state_bill["store_macros"] = silicon["state_sram_macros"], and both the unit test and the shipped validator then assert those two are equal. Both pass unconditionally.

    Underneath sits a real divergence: fws_eval.py:3316 ceils each stage to whole macros and sums, while tools/fws_qif_dse.py:876 ceils the pooled total once — contradicting state_sram_composition's own contract ("the caller sums the stages"). Same bill, two published answers: 12,940 macros / 685.90 mm² in the report against 12,933 / 685.53 mm² in the sweep. Stages sit on different chips, so a macro cannot straddle two; the pooled ceil under-charges by up to (stages − 1) macros. No shipped point flips — but the gate that would catch a future one is inert.

  5. digital_area_mm2_total omits the whole state storemajor

    fws_eval.py:4645, docstring at :4564

    The evaluator's digital total is chiplet_area × chiplets + pool_area × slots, under a docstring claiming "every digital mm² and watt … ONE ACCOUNTING (D21)". At the parent commit it agreed with the DSE; this change set added the store to one side only. The shipped markdown report prints 319.551 mm² three lines below the line naming a 685.532 mm² store. The correct figure is 1005.45. The store's composed leakage (0.4194 W) is read by nothing anywhere, so DECISIONS.md:262 ("only the macro's LEAKAGE power is carried") is false as written.

  6. built_duty_max is the duty of a chiplet that is not builtmajor

    tools/fws_qif_dse.py:790, prose at :1245

    Computed from per-stage loads on the premise "one chiplet per stage". Granite at shared_chiplets = 2 publishes built_duty_max: 0.2431; the two chiplets actually built run at 0.5061 and 0.5063 — understated 2.08×. The same premise is written into the shipped prose: candidate c002 says "the 10 shared digital chiplets this machine builds (one per stage)" over 20 stages.

  7. The DSE writes one home of the slot count and refuses the other by neither name nor valuemajor

    tools/fws_qif_dse.py:733/738 · :230 · :2659

    mapping.macros_per_chip is a legal, documented field and is not in REFUSED_AXES. Declare it co-consistently in a frontier config and four of five candidates die at stage mapping with "macros_per_chip = 386 disagrees with arrays_per_chip = 193 … (D21)" — a disagreement the tool wrote one line earlier and blames on the user. The surviving candidate is the one whose derived capacity happens to match the stale declaration, and the report then publishes "flat_area: every valid candidate carries the SAME total silicon" for a sweep that in fact spans 541.6 to 561.2 mm². emit_selected_config shares the blind spot, so the "COMPLETE RUNNABLE" emitted YAML is itself un-runnable whenever the derived count differs.

  8. The state-residency disclosure names a config field and prints something elsemajor

    fws_eval.py:3130, :3147, :3157, :3166, comment at :3122

    The row labels capacity_bytes — now the built store — as tech_param.SRAM-L2.size. A fresh Granite run prints "the ON-CHIP reading against tech_param.SRAM-L2.size (3.148e+07 B)" on one stage and "(6.044e+07 B)" on another, in one document, against a declared 64 MB. The text also says the verdict "is taken against" that tier; the verdict is the constant "sized". The relaxation is still named stage_state_tier_is_one_declared_size, and the justifying comment cites max_stage_state_bytes — a key the DSE refuses by name. This text is live, not stale: it regenerates into every report.

  9. One absent block, two behavioursmajor

    fws_eval.py:3232  vs  tools/fws_qif_dse.py:876

    The evaluator swallows SynthesisLibraryError with a bare except Exception and returns 0 macros / 0.0 mm² with the stage verdict still "sized" and the prose still asserting the store is "charged as silicon". The DSE raises on the same condition. Point the Granite card at the repo's 12nm library — which has no SRAM macro — and the report path completes silently with 685.9 mm² of store vanished while the sweep path exits 2. The page renders capacity_bytes under the header "store built", so a reader cannot tell a composed store from a swallowed refusal. A measured zero and an absent law are byte-identical here — which is the one thing the honesty rules forbid.

  10. The decisions register is half-updatedmajor

    docs/qif/DECISIONS.md:283, :298, :306

    ADJ-12 records "241,362 tokens/s at 14,788 mm²" for Falcon-Mamba; the shipped sweep selects 1,240,208.88 tokens/s at 8,559.05 mm², and neither recorded number appears in any candidate of any shipped sweep. ViT is recorded as "1 chip, 400 slots, 386 tiles, 779 requests/s" against a shipped 386 slots / 789.84 req/s — and 386 tiles in 400 slots is exactly the unowned-slot waste ADJ-13 claims two sections later to have eliminated. ADJ-14's numbers match today's artifacts exactly; within one ADJ-11 sentence the areas are current and the throughputs are not.

  11. The demo sweep cannot be regeneratedminor

    configs/hardware-config/fws_cim_granite_tiny_dse.yaml:273 · docs/fws_cim/README.md:416

    The config declares arrays_per_chip as an axis, which is now refused by name, and the README still publishes that exact command writing to a directory this change set renamed. The checked-in report can no longer be re-derived from its own config.

  12. Saturation guard measures the wrong chipletminor

    cim_timing.py:5059, :5070 · duplicated at :4674/:4711

    The measure handed to _grow is the max group time while the loop predicate is a per-group deficit. A group that is flat in width but absolutely slowest freezes the search, and the still-failing group is declared unreachable. Reproduced: the emitted line reads "UNREACHABLE at any width", with both numbers taken from the chiplet that meets its target, while another group sits 2× over its pace and would reach it at 2× width. Latent — every shipped stack has uniform per-stage pace, so this needs a heterogeneous layers_per_stage.

    This is the failure mode I asked the lane to hunt for, and it is real — just not for the reason I guessed. The plateau is not the problem; measuring the wrong group is.

  13. The fabric's ADJ-14 verdict is computed and droppedminor

    cim_timing.py:4716, surviving text at :4761

    fabric_shared_widened is assigned and never read; DerivedFabricSizing has no field for it, so the fabric never emits the ADJ-14 disclosure its engine twin emits. Qwen at shared_chiplets = 2 produces target_reached: False with the shared group 56× over its analog pace, and the run says nothing. Where the widening does move the width, the surviving ADJ-10 text still claims the count is "the smallest … for which every stage's attention time fits that stage's own ANALOG m-pass time", which is then false.

Plausible — argued, not reproduced end to end

  • The size of the missing conv pool silicon. Two independent reproductions disagree: one computed ~58 mm², the other ran compose_macro_pool and got 0.404 mm² per macro against the shipped 0.0024 — about 1,449 mm² over 3,584 slots, 17% of Falcon-Mamba's total. cim_timing.py:2276 adds an FP_MULT and an FP_ADD per conv lane, which favours the larger figure. No gate flips at 20,000 mm² either way; the reported term is wrong regardless.
  • Whether the stage_state_kv memory verdict can ever be the decisive refuser. In every reproduction the per-chip activation_sram+kv row violated first. What is certainly wrong is the justifying comment, which is item 8 above.

What held

The max_silicon_mm2 gate is correct — _silicon does include the store and the page renders the right total. The four shipped hardware cards do equal their sweeps' selected_config.yaml. The OPTIMA validator is green at 525/525. The shipped atlas JSON for Falcon-Mamba and ViT reproduces byte-identically from the emitter, so the artifacts are current and the decisions register is the stale side. The suspected unguarded attention dereference at base_timing.py:152 is not a defect: attention is a dataclass field that is None rather than absent.

Unaudited

The validator changed 129 lines and only its ADJ-11 gate was read; the rewritten ADJ-13 and ADJ-14 gates were not. ViT and Falcon-Mamba have no gate coverage anywhere — not in the validator, not in the frontier tests; nobody ran --verify on their frontier configs and diffed against the checked-in reports. ViT maps to zero pipeline stages, so the chiplet grouping returns empty and ADJ-14, the sized store and D are all degenerate on it; its state_sram_provenance: "absent" is missing from uncovered_terms while the basis string names it — expect a real honesty-rule defect there, unexamined. Nothing checked the energy and power laws, the MoE and expert-parallel paths, program/fws_build.py beyond the two placement lines, or the results page beyond spot checks.
