Plan P7

Folding & the Mapping Optimizer

Never waste weight space, pin analog at the cell floor, and sweep the (area, decode throughput) frontier by sizing the whole system — with the dead folds refused by name before any machinery exists for them.

Mission context

The QIF core question, stated by George 2026-08-24: the hardware is FIXED (macros of one geometry, chips of one capacity); how you program and connect it is flexible. Under Invariant W every bank holds real weights, so the mapping freedom is WHICH tensors' blocks co-reside where (K-stacks with accumulators, cross-tensor bank sharing, chip locality) and how the system around the analog floor is provisioned. Choosing that, per model, is the project's center. The Wave D DSE proved the gap by construction: its Pareto front collapsed to one point and its own front_shape note said why — nothing swept traded silicon for speed. Folding is the missing axis.

The invariant and the three facts that define the problem

Invariant W (George, 2026-08-24, HARD): weight space is NEVER wasted. Every mux bank of every macro holds real weights — a "spatial" mapping does not idle banks, it fills them with other projections, layers, or tensors. Waste exists only at dimension-mismatch remainders and is a REPORTED metric (target ~0). Consequence: analog_macros ~= ceil(total_model_cells / cells_per_macro) — the GLOBAL cell floor. Analog silicon is determined by the model, not chosen by the mapper.

Fact 1 — at the floor, decode analog time is assignment-invariant to first order. Dense packing means every bank fires once per decode step (each weight read once per token), so each macro walks ~m ADC passes per step regardless of which tensors co-reside. Packing still chooses two SECONDARY things: whether a tensor's K-blocks stack in one macro (priced accumulator + buffer) or spread across macros (partial-sum transport), and which chip holds what (activation locality). Both are optimized greedily (keep K-blocks together, keep a layer on one chip); neither is the frontier.

Fact 2 — the frontier is SYSTEM SIZING. The observed 0.06% analog duty on Granite was a diagnosis of a mis-provisioned config (10 analog chips against a starved scan engine), not a law. With analog pinned at the floor, the DSE's real levers are the DIGITAL side: shared chiplet count, vector lanes, SA geometry, pool widths. Provision digital up until the step time falls and every device class is well utilized; the knee is where the analog m-pass time becomes co-bound with the digital side. THE OBJECTIVE: sweep the (total area = analog floor + digital silicon, decode tokens/s) frontier, with per-device utilization reported at every point so idle silicon is always visible. NO safety margins anywhere (George).

Fact 3 — decode sequentiality keeps co-residency free. Layers execute sequentially within a decode step, so cross-tensor and cross-layer bank sharing never contends; the evaluator prices this correctly by construction. Prefill breaks this (concurrent layers, SSD chunking) and is OUT (D25).

The investigation

1. Dense packing (Invariant W in code)

The packer's contract: fill every bank with real weights; report waste% (remainder columns/rows from dimension mismatch) as a first-class metric. K-blocks of one tensor stack in-macro by preference (one accumulator per output bank, K-inner walk, priced buffer); a layer's tensors stay on one chip by preference (locality); overflow spills to the next macro/chip, never idles. The degenerate identity: today's shipped configs, re-expressed as dense packings, reproduce their numbers bit-exactly where the placement coincides, and the delta is disclosed where dense packing changes macro count.

2. The floor law and the analog step time

analog_macros = ceil(total_cells / cells_per_macro) per model (+ endpoint terms); decode analog step time ~= m × vec_latency × (sequential chain depth effects), assignment-invariant at the floor. Accumulator and partial-transport costs ride the affected ops (P2 laws). Utilization per device class is a required output of every run.

3. The system-sizing DSE (the core loop)

Analog fixed at the floor; sweep the DIGITAL provisioning axes — shared chiplet count, vector_lanes, SA geometry (rows × cols × num_arrays), pool widths — plus chip-count granularity, through the mapped path (fws_qif_dse). Output: the (total area, decode tokens/s) Pareto frontier with per-device utilization at every point. The knee — where digital provisioning stops buying throughput because the analog m-pass time co-binds — is the deliverable figure per model. No safety margin; the frontier itself shows how close each point runs.

4. The visualization (the part George asked for by name)

  • Folding card: one tensor, the mapping family side by side — spatial boxes, time-lanes stacked inside a box, the accumulator drawn where K-partials meet; area/time/energy under each.
  • Macro time-lane view (atlas drill-in): a macro's mux slots as time lanes showing which (K,N) block or which other tensor occupies each pass.
  • The knee explorer: area vs tokens/s per model, every point a complete fold assignment, clickable through to its atlas.
