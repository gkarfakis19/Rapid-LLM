Plan P7

Folding & the Mapping Optimizer

Make spatial-vs-temporal folding a per-tensor mapping decision, and choose it optimally per model — decode only, with the dead folds refused by name before any machinery exists for them.

Mission context

The QIF core question, stated by George 2026-08-24: the hardware is FIXED (macros of one geometry, chips of one capacity); how you program and connect it is flexible. A K×N tensor on r×c macros with mux m admits a family of mappings from fully spatial (many macros, banks idle, parallel time) to fully temporal (few macros, banks full, folded time with shift-add accumulation). Choosing the folding per tensor, per model, is the project's center. The Wave D DSE proved the gap by construction: its Pareto front collapsed to one point and its own front_shape note said why — nothing swept traded silicon for speed. Folding is the missing axis.

The two facts that prune the space

Fact 1 — folding never changes cell count. Weights own cells (D2): a 1024×1024 tensor needs 220 cells whether it maps to 4 macros or 1. A "2×2 spatial" mapping buys 4 fixed macros and leaves their mux banks IDLE to buy parallel time; folding fills the banks the spatial mapping wastes. Therefore min_macros(tensor) = ceil(tensor_cells / cells_per_macro) — the CELL FLOOR — and the whole per-tensor mapping space is one small integer: how far between the floor and full-spatial the tensor sits. Area = macros bought; time multiplier = temporal folds walked; there is no separate taxonomy of folds to build machinery for.

Fact 2 — decode makes the optimizer nearly analytic. In the Granite decode timeline the analog macros run at ~0.06% duty: the step is bound by the shared digital chiplet (scan for hybrids, attention/KV for transformers). And within one decode step the layers execute SEQUENTIALLY, so tiles of different layers sharing one macro's banks NEVER contend — the evaluator already prices this as zero by construction. Consequence: fold depth is limited only by each tensor's FOLD HEADROOM (fold until its analog time approaches the binding step time), and cross-layer bank sharing is a pure area win. The decode optimizer is headroom computation plus bank packing, not search. Prefill is where folding gets hard (all layers fire concurrently, SSD chunking interacts with fold walks) — which is exactly why it is out.

The investigation

1. The fold vector (P7's one new object)

Per tensor: fold = (f_K, f_N) with f_K ≤ ceil(K/r), f_N ≤ ceil(N/c), and the bank-capacity constraint (blocks / (f_K · f_N)) · banks_per_block ≤ mux per macro. (1,1) = today's fully spatial mapping (degenerate identity: bit-exact reproduction of current numbers, gated). Area = macros bought; time = analog M-law × f_K · f_N; energy adds the accumulator term for f_K > 1. New card parameters (Samyak seam, honest defaults): accumulator width/energy per fold, input re-drive cost, bank-switch cycles (knob exists, default 0 disclosed).

2. The decode headroom law

headroom(tensor) = binding_step_time × safety / analog_time_at_(1,1), read off the existing decode timeline. Fold to min(cell-floor-fold, headroom). On Granite the aggregate analog time is ~12.8 µs of a ~892 µs step — headroom O(70×) — so the cell floor, not time, binds almost everywhere. The knee where folded analog time meets the scan bound is the deliverable figure.

3. The optimizer (deliberately not a search)

Objective pass one: MIN AREA at a declared tokens/s target; the Pareto traced by sweeping the target. Per-tensor folds from the headroom law, then first-fit-decreasing bank packing with cross-layer sharing (contention-free in decode; the evaluator re-verifies every chosen point — the two-speed pattern). Every chosen mapping is emitted as a normal mapping: + cim.allocation config: the optimizer WRITES configs, the existing machinery validates, prices and draws them. No new evaluator, no ML.

4. The visualization (the part George asked for by name)

  • Folding card: one tensor, the mapping family side by side — spatial boxes, time-lanes stacked inside a box, the accumulator drawn where K-partials meet; area/time/energy under each.
  • Macro time-lane view (atlas drill-in): a macro's mux slots as time lanes showing which (K,N) block or which other tensor occupies each pass.
  • The knee explorer: area vs tokens/s per model, every point a complete fold assignment, clickable through to its atlas.
