Plan P2

The Macro Resource Model

Make the analog macro a device-generic, tiled, bit-sliced resource that the DAG executor can price.

Mission context

The macro is the atomic resource (A3). Every claim the framework makes about time, energy, and area bottoms out in the laws on this page. The macro is also the binding constraint of the whole project: weights own cells, so area decides what fits (D2).

Two shifts define the work. The macro stops being one model's array and becomes a shared surface that hosts tiles (A3, D10). The macro stops being CTT and becomes a card (D14). Everything else here follows from those two.

Scope & exclusions

In: the device card; the tile as allocation unit; banking and mux allocation, priced and validated; bit-slicing and its digital reduction (D11); the per-macro digital pool (D12); occupancy semantics for the DAG executor; analog energy and area law shapes.

Out:

  • Attention and every activation×activation op EXECUTION. Those ops run on the shared digital chiplet (D13), placed by P3 and priced by P4 — but the chiplet's DEVICE CARD and its laws (the relocated SA and softmax-lane laws, the scan laws to come) live here: one law module owns every device card (audit resolution 2026-08-23; George may overrule).
  • Which tile goes on which macro. P2 prices and validates an allocation; P3 chooses it.
  • DAG evaluation, metrics, and reports (P4).
  • Accuracy. NEVER a factor anywhere — performance model only (D23).
  • Partial weight stationarity (D9) and weight writes. Fixed weights admit no writes; this is permanent, not phased.

Where we stand

The physics already exists in validated form. What is missing is identity: today an array is a count, not a thing with an owner and a site.

OPTIMA's banking is the clearest statement of the gap. _apply_partial_banking smears an area and energy delta across layers, leaves per-stage numbers fully unbanked by its own comment, asserts that banking does not change vector timing, and prices bank switching at zero (AUDIT finding 3). D10 and D11 exist to replace that with real allocation, real timing, and a real switch cost.

The investigation

1. Device cards

A card is a parameter set plus structural knobs. The parameters set the numbers. The knobs change the shape of the laws, so the tool must read them, not assume them. CTT is the first card. ReRAM and MRAM are cards, not rewrites (D14).

GroupFieldsRole
Geometryrows, cols, muxStored columns = cols × mux. Sets tile capacity.
Timingslice_cycles, analog_clock, ADC latencyFeeds the per-op vector latency.
Energyenergy_per_vec_pj, shots_per_output, shot kindPer-vector analog energy. Shot kind decides whether shots also cost time.
Areaarea_mm2_per_array, peripheral area (ADC and mux)Macro footprint, split so one accounting stays one accounting (D21).
Structuralbits_per_cell, slicing arrangement, bank/mux depth, 3D stack heightThe knobs. Each one changes a law, not just a constant.
AccuracyRemoved (D23)Accuracy is never a factor; the card carries no accuracy field.
ValidityAdmitted (bits-per-cell, weight-bits, mux) pointsThe menu. The tool refuses an unlisted point instead of interpolating.

The validity menu is the honesty device. A device does not support every bits-per-cell; a card that pretends otherwise produces numbers nobody can defend.

2. The tile as allocation unit

A tile is one weight sub-matrix resident in one macro, with a name. A macro hosts tiles from the same layer, from other layers, from other experts, or from other models (A3). Co-residency is the normal case, not a multi-tenancy feature bolted on later.

FieldMeaning
OwnerModel, layer, op, expert, shard. Every tile has a named owner (D21).
ShapeThe K range and N range of the weight matrix it holds.
SliceWhich bit slice of the weight word (see 3).
SiteMacro id, row range, column-set (bank) ids.

The existing census law becomes the tile enumerator. ceil(K/rows) × ceil(N/(cols × mux)) still counts the units; placement gives each unit an owner and a site. Capacity is then a real check: the column sets claimed by all resident tiles must fit the macro's stored columns.

Allocation follows D10 in four steps: the user writes it in config, the tool prices and validates it, the DSE sweeps it, and the mapper automates it later. P2 owns the first two. P3 owns the fourth.

3. Bit-slicing, natively priced

When bits-per-cell is below the weight bit width, an active column set yields a partial result. The number of slices is n_s = ceil(weight_bits / bits_per_cell). Full results compose through a digital shift-and-add tree that costs real area, real time, and real energy (D11). The composition point is a node in the DAG, never a hidden constant.

ArrangementWhere partials liveWhat it costs
Column sets, one macron_s column sets per logical output columnStored-column demand × n_s; one ADC pass per set; a local tree in the macro's digital pool.
Chained macrosOne slice per macroMacro count × n_s; partial bytes on the p2p law (D17); the tree sits at the sink.
Temporal (input bit-serial)Successive shotsTime × input slices. Only if the card declares temporal shots.

Law shapes, not final equations:

  • Tile and stored-column demand scale with n_s. Area follows directly.
  • ADC work scales with n_s. Sharing one ADC across slices trades area for time. That trade is a Samyak question, not a guess.
  • The reduction tree has depth about log2(n_s), area about (n_s - 1) adders per output lane, and energy about (n_s - 1) adds per output. Pipelined, it returns one result per cycle after fill.
  • The chained form adds a transport term and a longer critical path. It buys back stored columns in the source macro.

The final equations get pinned when the card menus arrive. Committing to them now would fix numbers we do not have.

4. The per-macro digital pool

Each macro carries a small digital pool (D12). It holds the shift-and-add trees, the partial accumulators for chained slices, activations, elementwise work, and bias or scale application. It does not hold attention or any other activation×activation compute; that goes to the shared digital chiplet (D13).

QuestionAnswer
Sizing ruleThe pool consumes at least the macro's peak result rate, so it never blocks.
Where the size comes fromDerived from the card and the resident tiles, not configured.
Status of the numberDerived and REPORTED. Not a DSE constraint for now (D12).
Revisit triggerThe pool stops being absorbed when its derived area or energy passes a disclosed share of the macro it serves. At that point it becomes a priced, contended resource.

This is the direct heir of the pass-1 helper-absorption contract, with one change: absorption was an assumption, and the pool is a computed number that the report prints. The threshold is a proposal awaiting George (see open questions).

5. Occupancy semantics

The macro is a serializing resource. The DAG executor prices ops onto it. There is no period law (A1).

QuestionAnswer
What serializesThe ADC and mux path. One stored column set converts at a time.
Op durationM_tokens × active_column_sets × slice_cycles / f_analog. Still free of K and N.
ContentionCo-resident tiles queue on their macro. The stall appears in the DAG, not in a formula.
PeriodNone. Throughput is a DAG result (A1, P4).
Reduction identityOne owner filling every mux slot pays mux × slice_cycles — the pass-1 charge, exactly.

The change is that an op pays for the column sets it activates rather than the full mux depth. Pass 1 charged the full depth because the whole macro belonged to one matrix. Under A3 that is a special case, and it still reproduces itself. Co-residency then costs the right thing: capacity is shared spatially, and the ADC path is shared serially.

6. Energy and area laws

Energy per op is the analog term over the activated sets, plus the reduction tree, plus the pool, plus transport when slices cross macros. Area is the resident arrays, plus the peripheral (ADC and mux), plus the pool. 3D stack height divides the footprint that a macro occupies on the package; it does not divide silicon or energy, and it brings TSV and thermal limits the card must declare.

One honesty rule governs both (D21): one accounting per metric. Every reported area and energy figure has exactly one definition and one place it comes from. Today's PARTIAL energy label stays until the pool and peripheral terms land.

Seams

Imports

  • P1 — weight-matrix shapes per supported model, weight and activation bit widths, expert counts, and which ops are weight GEMMs rather than activation×activation.
  • P3 — the tile-to-macro allocation to price and validate, including owner ids, slice groups, and chained-macro chains.
  • P4 — the op set the DAG executor will hand to a macro, and the shape of the duration and energy values it expects back.
  • P6 — the parity targets that constrain every law change, and the honesty invariants (one accounting per metric, disclosed relaxations).

Exports

  • The device card schema, the CTT card, and named empty slots for ReRAM and MRAM.
  • The tile object: owner, shape, slice, site.
  • The tile enumerator and the macro capacity and validity checks (for P3).
  • Per-op pricing: given an op, its tiles, and its token count, return duration, energy, and the resource it serializes on (for P4).
  • Bit-slice composition: slice groups plus the reduction ops the DAG must contain (for P3 and P4).
  • Derived per-macro digital pool sizing, with its area and energy, reported.
  • Macro occupancy semantics: serializing resource, active column sets, no period.
  • Analog area and energy accounting, one definition per metric (for P4, P5, P6).

Open questions for George

  1. Shared digital chiplet card — audit-resolved, confirm. The draft found no owner for the chiplet's device model (P2 said not-me, P3 said P4, P4 said P2 — a circle). The audit resolved it by the one-law-module rule: P2 owns the card and its laws; P3 places, P4 prices. Confirm or overrule.
  2. The pool revisit trigger. Proposal: the pool stops being absorbed when its derived area exceeds a fifth of the macro footprint it serves, or its energy exceeds the analog energy of the op it serves. Confirm or set your own share.
  3. Is slicing the default or the exception? If CTT normally runs bits-per-cell below the weight width, slicing is the main path and the reduction tree is always present. If it is the exception, slicing stays opt-in.
  4. Active-set charging vs the parity gates. The per-op charge changes a number only when a macro is under-occupied. Confirm that the OPTIMA parity points stay on full-occupancy configurations, so the gates hold unmoved.
  5. Allocation granularity. Is the mux slot the smallest allocatable unit? A finer unit complicates the tile object, the validation, and the atlas (P5).
  6. Second card now or later? One plausible ReRAM or MRAM card would prove the schema is genuinely device-generic. It also invites numbers we cannot defend yet.
